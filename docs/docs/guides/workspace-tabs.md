---
title: Arrange the editor in tabs
---

The bar under the editor's title bar holds **tabs**, each a whole layout of panels. One is
always shown. The first, **3D**, holds the Scene panel and the panels around it; it can't be
closed.

## Open a tab

Press **+** on the bar, choose **File > New Tab...** or press **Ctrl+T**, then pick:

- **3D Scene** goes to the tab holding the Scene panel.
- **2D Scene** opens the **2D Scene** panel: the world seen flat from the front, X to the
  right and Y up, as a 2D game sees it. Scroll to zoom, drag with the right or middle button
  to pan, and click an entity to select it. Drag it to move it; with **Snap** on it lands on
  the grid. **Ctrl+Z** undoes a move.
- **UI Scene** opens the UI Designer (see [UI scenes](ui-scenes.md)).

**File > New 3D Scene Tab**, **New 2D Scene Tab** and **New UI Scene Tab** do the same.
**Ctrl+Tab** and **Ctrl+Shift+Tab** step through the tabs.

## Make a tab of any panels

Drag a panel by its tab up onto the bar. Let go on a tab to move the panel into it, or on
**Drop here for a new tab** to give it a tab of its own. You could, for example, give the
Inspector a whole tab. A tab closes by itself when its last panel leaves.

## Pop a tab out

Right-click a tab and choose **Pop out into window**, or drag it down off the bar. The tab
becomes a window of its own that you can dock panels in, like the main one. Click its tab on
the bar to bring it back. **Close tab** (or the tab's **x**, or a middle-click) closes it: the
Inspector, Hierarchy and other usual panels go back to the 3D tab.

Your tabs are kept with the rest of the layout, so they are there the next time you open the
editor.
