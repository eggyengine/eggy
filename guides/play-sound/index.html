Play sound and 3D audio

Eggy plays WAV, FLAC, MP3 and Ogg Vorbis files. A sound can come from a place in the world, so it pans left and right and gets quieter with distance from the listener, or it can play unplaced, like music or a menu click. Put files anywhere in resources/. They ship with the game as they are.

Listen in the editor​

Select a sound in the Assets panel. The Asset Preview shows its waveform, how long it is, and whether it is mono or stereo. Press Play to hear it, Pause to hold it, and Stop to end it and go back to the start. Click or drag along the waveform to play from that point. Turn on Loop to hear it repeat.

Put a sound on an entity​

  1. Select an entity and add an AudioSource component in the Inspector.
  2. Drag a sound from Assets onto its sound slot.
  3. Press Play. The sound starts with the game.
FieldWhat it does
playingThe sound plays while this is on. A once sound turns it off when it ends. Turn it on again to replay.
repeatonce or loop.
gainVolume: 1 as recorded, 0 silent.
pitchPlayback rate: 2 is an octave up and twice as fast.
busWhich volume group it belongs to: music, effects, dialogue, ui or master.
spatialOn: it sounds from the entity's place. Off: it plays unplaced (use this for music).
min_distance_mWithin this many metres it plays at full volume.
max_distance_mPast this many metres it gets no quieter.
rolloffHow fast it fades between the two. 0 doesn't fade.

Placed sounds follow their entity as it moves, including when it's moved by a parent. Sources only play while the game runs: not while you edit the scene, and they hold still while the game is paused.

Choose where the player hears from​

Placed sounds are heard from the listener. Add an AudioListener component to the entity that should hear, usually the player or the camera. It hears from its position and faces the way the entity faces (its -z). Without an AudioListener, the active camera is the listener.

Play sounds from code​

app.audio() plays a file and returns a Voice, which you can then change or stop:

const jump = eggy.AssetRef(.audio).init("resources/sounds/jump.ogg");
const boom = eggy.AssetRef(.audio).init("resources/sounds/boom.wav");

pub fn update(self: *Game, app: *eggy.App) !void {
    // Unplaced, at half volume.
    if (app.input.pressed(.space)) _ = try app.audio().play(jump, .{ .gain = 0.5 });

    // From a place in the world, fading out by 30 metres.
    if (self.exploded) _ = try app.audio().play(boom, .{
        .position_m = .{ 4, 0, -2 },
        .falloff = .{ .min_distance_m = 2, .max_distance_m = 30 },
    });
}

A .once voice ends by itself. Keep the Voice to control a sound while it plays:

self.siren = try app.audio().play(siren, .{ .repeat = .loop, .position_m = car_position });
// Every frame, as the car moves:
try app.audio().setPosition(self.siren, car_position);
// Later:
try app.audio().stop(self.siren);
CallWhat it does
play(sound, options)Starts a sound. The options are gain, pitch, repeat, position_m, falloff, bus (effects unless you choose another) and start_s.
pause(voice) / unpause(voice)Holds a sound where it is, then lets it go on.
stop(voice)Ends a sound.
setGain, setPitch, setPositionChange a playing sound. setPosition places an unplaced sound.
position(voice) / seek(voice, seconds)Where a sound is, in seconds, and moving it. play can also start part way in, with start_s.
setRepeat(voice, repeat)Makes a playing sound loop, or end when it next reaches its end.
isPlaying(voice)Whether it's still going. A paused sound counts as playing.
setBusGain(bus, gain)Sets one bus's volume, or everything's with .master. Use it for volume sliders.
setListener(position, forward, up)Places the listener yourself, in scenes with no AudioListener and no active camera.
hold(true) / hold(false)Holds every sound at once, for a pause menu.

Once a voice ends, calls on it do nothing and isPlaying returns false. Every sound stops when you press Play or Stop, and when the editor reloads your code.

Up to 64 sounds play at once. Past that, play returns error.TooManyVoices. Fix a file that won't decode (error.UnreadableSound) by exporting it again as WAV or Ogg Vorbis.

If the computer has no audio device, the game still runs. Calls still work, but nothing can be heard.
